Which AI should I use?

ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity, Copilot, Meta AI… You don't need all of them. Here's how to pick, in plain English, plus a directory of 36 useful AI tools.

The short answer

  1. Just starting?Pick ChatGPT. It's the easiest place to begin and does a bit of everything.
  2. Live in Gmail, Google Docs or Android?Use Gemini. It works inside the Google apps you already have.
  3. Write a lot, or read long documents?Use Claude. It's the best I've tested for clear writing and long, careful work.

All three have a free plan. Start free, and only pay when you hit the limit every day.

The main ones

ChatGPT

Best for: getting started, everyday questions, voice chats, images.

Good to know: the most popular, so most tutorials use it.

Gemini

Best for: anyone on Google: Gmail, Docs, Drive, Photos and Android phones.

Good to know: Gemini Live is great for talking things through out loud.

Claude

Best for: writing that sounds human, long documents, careful thinking, and building things.

Good to know: use Projects to give it your files and instructions once.

Perplexity, Copilot, Meta AI

Perplexity: a search engine that answers, with links to its sources.

Microsoft Copilot: for people who live in Word, Excel and Outlook.

Meta AI: already inside WhatsApp and Instagram, so it's handy for quick questions.

Start with one of these. They cover most daily tasks.

Start here
  • Pick one everyday assistant. For most people, that's ChatGPT.
  • Use it every day for a week on real work: emails, notes, a document you're stuck on.
  • Then add one specialist tool for the job you do most: video, slides, meetings or research.
  • Don't collect AI apps. Two tools you use daily beat ten you opened once.

Free or paid?

  • Start with the free plan. It's enough for most people.
  • Pay for one only when you hit the daily limit again and again. Most paid plans cost about $20 (around ₹2,000) a month.
  • Don't pay for three. Learning one well beats knowing three a little.
Stay safe
  • Never paste passwords, bank details, OTPs or ID numbers.
  • Double-check anything about health, money or the law with a real expert.
  • AI can sound sure and still be wrong. Ask it for sources.
