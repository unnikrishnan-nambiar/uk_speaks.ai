Why now

Big companies get hacked every month. Your email, phone number or password can end up on a leaked list. You may never know.

AI makes scams harder to spot. Scammers can now fake messages, voices and websites in seconds.

What's a breach? When hackers break into a company and steal its customers' details. The stolen list is called a “leak”.

You don't need to be good with tech. Each check says what it is, why it matters and how to do it. Too much? Do one check a day.

The 5 checks

01

Check 1: Check known breaches

What it is: Have I Been Pwned is a free website. It keeps a list of known leaks. Type your email. It shows which leaks it was in, and what was taken: passwords, phone numbers, addresses.

Why it matters: do you still use a leaked password? Then anyone with that list can get into your other accounts.

  1. Open the site and type your email address. Only your email, never a password.
  2. Read the list. For each breach, note what was leaked and when.
  3. Still using the password you had on that site? Change it now, on every account that uses it.
  4. Check every email you use, including old ones. Tap Notify me to get an email if you show up in a new breach.
  5. Use Chrome or Android? Google Password Checkup checks every password Google saved for you in one go.
  • haveibeenpwned.comIs my email in a known breach? Free, no sign-up.
  • Google Password CheckupFinds leaked, reused and weak passwords saved in Google.
02

Check 2: Remove unused app access

What it is: you tap “Sign in with Google”. Or you let an app “see your contacts”. That app keeps the access, often for years. Even after you stop using it.

Why it matters: if that app gets hacked or sold, the attacker gets that access too. Fewer apps means fewer doors in.

  1. Google: open Third-party connections. Remove every app you don't recognise or haven't used in months. Be extra careful with any app that can read your Gmail or Drive.
  2. Google devices: open Your devices and sign out of any phone or laptop that isn't yours anymore.
  3. WhatsApp: Settings → Linked devices. Log out of any computer you don't recognise. Someone can read all your chats from there.
  4. Instagram and Facebook: Settings → Accounts Center → Password and security → Where you're logged in. Log out of anything unknown.
  5. Finish with Google's Security Checkup. It walks you through the rest.
  • Google: Third-party connectionsSee and remove apps with access to your Google account.
  • Google: Your devicesSign out of old or unknown phones and laptops.
  • Google Security CheckupA 5-minute check of everything on your account.
03

Check 3: Unique passwords + turn on 2FA

What's a password manager? A safe place on your phone. It makes a strong password for each account and remembers it for you.

What's 2FA? Two-factor authentication. A second lock: a code or a tap on your phone when someone logs in on a new device.

Why it matters: one password everywhere means one leak opens everything. With 2FA on, a leaked password is not enough to get in.

  1. Pick one password manager. The free one already on your phone is fine: Google Password Manager (Android, Chrome) or the Passwords app (iPhone).
  2. Change the passwords of your important accounts first: email, bank, UPI apps, WhatsApp, Instagram. Let the manager suggest a strong one.
  3. Turn on 2-Step Verification in Google. Choose a passkey or a Google prompt on your phone over SMS codes if you can.
  4. WhatsApp: Settings → Account → Two-step verification. Set a 6-digit PIN and add your email.
  5. Instagram: Accounts Center → Password and security → Two-factor authentication.
  6. Save your Google backup codes somewhere safe, offline. They get you back in if you lose your phone.
  • Google Password ManagerFree, built into Chrome and Android.
  • BitwardenFree password manager that works on every phone and computer.
  • Google 2-Step VerificationTurn on 2FA for your Google account.
04

Check 4: Personal data in a separate account, with no AI access

What it is: a second Gmail account, only for your important papers. Aadhaar, PAN, passport, bank statements, insurance, certificates. Never use it to sign up for apps. Never connect it to an AI tool.

Why it matters: AI tools can now read your Gmail and Drive. That's useful. But if something goes wrong, everything in that account is open. A trick hidden inside an email can even fool the AI. So keep your most private files where no AI or app can reach.

  1. Create a new Google account. Use a strong, unique password and turn on 2-Step Verification straight away.
  2. Move your ID cards, bank documents and certificates into its Drive. Then delete them from your everyday account, including from Trash.
  3. Don't connect it to ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or any other AI app. If an app asks to “connect Gmail” or “connect Drive”, say no.
  4. Don't use it for “Sign in with Google” anywhere. No apps, no shopping sites.
  5. For Indian IDs, use DigiLocker, the government's official document wallet. Share a masked Aadhaar (only the last 4 digits show) when someone asks for a copy.
  • Create a Google accountYour separate, private documents account.
  • DigiLockerOfficial Government of India wallet for Aadhaar, PAN, licence and more.
  • myAadhaar (UIDAI)Download a masked Aadhaar to share instead of the full one.
05

Check 5: Never share an OTP. Verify first.

What it is: the most common scam. A call, SMS or WhatsApp says your account is hacked or blocked. Then it asks for the OTP you just got, “to fix it”. Some sound like your bank, the police or family. AI can copy voices now.

What's an OTP? One-time password. The short code your bank or app sends you by SMS. It's like a key. Never give it to anyone.

Why it matters: the OTP is the key. Whoever asks for it is trying to log in to your account or take money from it, right now. No real bank, company or police officer will ever ask for your OTP, PIN or password.

  1. Never share an OTP, PIN, password or screen, with anyone, for any reason. Don't install an app someone asks you to install on a call.
  2. Got a scary message? Hang up. Open the official app yourself, or call the number on the back of your card or on the official website.
  3. Don't click links in SMS or WhatsApp messages about “KYC”, “blocked account”, “parcel”, “electricity bill” or “refund”.
  4. A family member calls asking for urgent money? Hang up and call them back on their usual number. Agree on a family code word.
  5. Lost money? Call 1930 immediately, the national cybercrime helpline, and report on cybercrime.gov.in. The faster you report, the better the chance of stopping the payment.
  6. Report fraud calls and messages on Sanchar Saathi (Chakshu).
  • cybercrime.gov.inReport cyber fraud. Helpline: 1930.
  • Sanchar SaathiReport fraud calls and SMS (Chakshu), and see the mobile numbers in your name.

Set up a password manager and turn on 2FA 4. Create a separate account for my personal documents 5. How to spot fake "account hacked" messages Use simple words and tell me exactly where to tap. Never paste these into any AI

Passwords, OTPs, PINs, card numbers, Aadhaar or bank account numbers. The AI doesn't need them to help you.

Stay safe every day

  • Update your phone and apps when asked. Most updates fix security holes.
  • Install apps only from the Play Store or App Store. Never an APK file someone sends you.
  • Lock your phone with a PIN or fingerprint, and turn on Find My Device.
  • Public Wi-Fi: no banking or shopping. Use mobile data instead.
  • Too good to be true? Prize, job offer, “investment doubling”: it's a scam.
  • Check again every 3 months. Put a reminder in your calendar.

Think you've been hacked?

  1. Change the password of your email first, from a device you trust. Every other account can be reset through it.
  2. Sign out of all other devices: Google “Your devices”, WhatsApp “Linked devices”, Instagram “Where you're logged in”.
  3. Turn on 2FA if it was off.
  4. Money involved? Call your bank to block the card or UPI, then call 1930.
  5. Tell your family and friends not to trust messages from your account for a few days.
