Prompt 1 · New CV

You are an expert CV writer and recruiter who knows how ATS (applicant tracking system) software screens CVs. I've attached my current CV and the job description for the role I'm applying to. If the job description isn't attached, I'll paste it below this prompt. Talk to me in [English / Manglish]. Write the CV itself in English. STEP 1 — CHECK MY CV Find the top 15 keywords in the job description: skills, tools, certifications and job-title words. Show me which ones my CV already has, which are missing, and which I have but describe in different words. Give my current CV a match score out of 100 and explain it in 3 lines. STEP 2 — ASK ME QUESTIONS Before you rewrite anything, ask me all your questions to fill the gaps in one numbered list, in one message, so I can answer them together. Ask at most 10. Focus on: - Each missing keyword: have I really done this? Where, and how? - Numbers for my biggest achievements: money, time saved, %, people, customers. - For my most important work: the Situation, the Task, what Action I took, and the Result. - Which jobs or projects matter most for this role. If I say I haven't done something, do not add it. STEP 3 — REWRITE MY CV - ATS-friendly layout: one column. No tables, text boxes, icons, photos or graphics. Standard headings: Summary, Skills, Experience, Projects, Education, Certifications, Languages. Contact details in the main text, not in a header. Make my email and LinkedIn clickable links. Put dates on the right of the title line, in one date format everywhere. - Use real bullet points. Bold the key terms and numbers in each bullet, so a recruiter can scan them. - A 3-line summary written for this job, using the exact job title from the description. - A skills section with the exact words from the job description, only for skills I really have. Write an acronym and its full name once, e.g. "SEO (Search Engine Optimisation)". - Every experience and project bullet in STAR format, as one bullet point of one or two lines, written as one sentence: start with an action verb and end with the result, with a number wherever I gave you one. Never write the labels "Situation", "Task", "Action" or "Result" in the CV. - If I only observed something, write "observed" and do not imply I did the work. - One page if I have under 8 years of experience, two pages at most otherwise. - Never invent jobs, degrees, skills, tools or numbers. If a bullet needs a number I didn't give you, write [ADD NUMBER]. STEP 4 — GIVE IT TO ME - The new CV as a Word file (.docx) I can download and edit, and also as plain text in this chat. - The new match score, and any keywords still missing, with the reason. - 3 things from my experience I should bring up in the interview for this job.

Example: one bullet

Why good CVs get rejected

It's often not your CV. Most large companies use ATS software to sort applications. It looks for the words in the job description. If your CV says the same thing in different words, it can drop down the list before a person ever reads it.

So every job needs its own version. That's what this prompt does in a few minutes.

Step by step

01

Step 1: Open Claude and paste the prompt

Open the Claude app or claude.ai and start a new chat. Copy Prompt 1 and paste it. In the line “Talk to me in [English / Manglish]”, keep the one you want. Don't send it yet.

02

Step 2: Attach your CV and the job description

  • Tap + and attach your current CV, as a PDF or Word file.
  • Add the job description: copy the full text from LinkedIn, Naukri or the company's site and paste it under the prompt. A screenshot works too.
  • Use the full description, not just the title. The keywords are in the details.
  • Now send it.
03

Step 3: Answer Claude's questions

Claude first shows what your CV is missing and gives it a score. Then it asks you all its questions in one list. Answer them together. The more specific you are, the better your new CV.

  • Give numbers: “cut report time from 2 days to 3 hours”, “handled 40 customers a day”, “team of 6”.
  • Name the tools: Excel, Tally, SAP, Canva, whatever you really used.
  • Haven't done it? Say so. Claude won't add it.
  • Don't know the number? Say “not sure”. You'll see [ADD NUMBER] to fill in later.
04

Step 4: Get your new CV

You get an ATS-friendly CV made for that job, as a Word file you can edit, with every bullet in STAR format. You also get a new match score and 3 things to bring up in the interview.

  • Read every line. It's your CV: change anything that doesn't sound like you.
  • Replace every [ADD NUMBER].
  • No file? Copy the text into Google Docs or Word.
  • Save it as Firstname-Lastname-CV. Upload the Word file, or a PDF saved from Word, if the site asks for PDF.
05

Step 5: Applying for another job?

Stay in the same chat, so Claude already knows your answers. Send Prompt 2 with the new job description.

Prompt 2 · Next job

Here's the job description for another role I'm applying to: [PASTE THE JOB DESCRIPTION] Make a new version of my CV for this job, following the same steps and rules as before. Only ask me about skills and keywords that are new in this job description. Give it to me as a Word file I can edit.

ATS-friendly: quick check

  • One column. No tables, text boxes, icons, photos or skill bars.
  • Standard headings: Summary, Skills, Experience, Projects, Education.
  • One page if you have under 8 years of experience. Real bullet points, and the key terms in bold.
  • Phone and email in the main text, not in the page header.
  • The exact words from the job description, where they're true for you.
  • A Word file, or a PDF saved from Word. Never a scan or a photo of your CV.
Be honest

Only add what you've really done. The ATS gets you to the interview, and the interviewer will ask about every line. A better CV gets you seen more often, but no CV can promise an interview.

Your privacy

Prefer not to share your phone number and address with an AI? Remove them before you attach your CV, and add them back to the final file.
