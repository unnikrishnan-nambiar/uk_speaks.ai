LinkedIn

Let Claude write your LinkedIn. You post.

The plugin to add

avi691/linkedin-agent-skill

Set it up

01

Option 1: In the Claude app (no code)

Needs a paid Claude plan (Pro or higher). Works on the web and the desktop app.

  1. Customize → Plugins → Add → Add marketplaceCustomize is in the left sidebar
  2. Add from a repositoryPaste the plugin name from above and click Sync. A warning shows for any plugin not made by Anthropic.
  3. Open “Linkedin agent”, click AddYou'll see all 11 skills inside
  4. Check it's thereCustomize → Plugins → Yours
02

Option 2: In Claude Code

Run these two commands inside Claude Code:

Claude Code

/plugin marketplace add avi691/linkedin-agent-skill /plugin install linkedin-agent

03

Option 3: On the free plan

Plugins need a paid plan, but you can still use one skill at a time. Open the skills folder on GitHub, open the skill you want (for example li-post), copy everything in its SKILL.md file, and paste it at the top of a new chat with your request. The humanizer's scripts won't run this way, but the writing works.

04

Step 4: Teach it your voice, once

Start a new chat and type /li to see all 11 skills. Start with this:

Your first post

/li-post I want to write about [YOUR IDEA]

It gives you three hook options and asks for three of your own posts. Paste the three that sound most like you. It then writes a voice.md file describing how you write.

Keep that voice: each new chat in the Claude app starts fresh. Ask Claude for the voice.md file, then add it to a Claude Project called “LinkedIn”. Every chat in that project will write like you.

The 11 skills

What each one does

TypeWhat you get
/li-postOne idea into a post: 3 hooks from 21 formulas, then the full draft
/li-carouselA slide-by-slide carousel and the PDF to upload
/li-repurposeOne video, newsletter or transcript into a week of posts
/li-commentTwo real comment options for someone else's post
/li-replyReplies to the comments on your post, most important first
/li-dmA connection note, a first message and two follow-ups
/li-inboxSorts your messages and drafts the replies worth sending
/li-planYour week: 4 posts, when to post, and 10 people to engage with
/li-profileScores your profile out of 100 and rewrites the weakest parts
/li-auditRanks your past posts by engagement and says what to stop
/li-humanRemoves stock AI words and em dashes, then scores the draft

You don't have to remember them. Just ask normally, like “write a LinkedIn post about…”, and Claude picks the right skill.

Important

It never reads LinkedIn for you. You paste in the post, the comments or the messages. Screenshots work too.

05

Step 5: Your weekly routine

  • Once a week: run /li-plan. Tell it what you do, your themes and what happened this week.
  • 10 minutes a day: /li-comment on 2–3 posts and /li-reply to your own comments.
  • Before anything goes out: run it through /li-human, then read every number yourself.
  • Links: put them in the first comment, not in the post.

Example: /li-human

The swap of stock words is automatic. Getting it to really sound like you takes a rewrite with your own details. If it doesn't have a real number, it leaves {{your number}} for you to fill in. Never post it with made-up numbers.

What it can do

  • Write posts, comments, replies, DMs and carousels in your voice
  • Plan your week: what to post, when, and who to engage with
  • Score your profile out of 100 and rewrite the weak parts
  • Clean stock AI words and em dashes out of any draft

What it can't do

  • Post, comment or send anything on LinkedIn. You paste every one, which keeps your account safe: auto-posting tools break LinkedIn's rules.
  • Read your feed, comments or inbox. You paste them in.
  • Run on autopilot. Nothing is connected to your account.
  • Promise you'll pass AI detectors. Its score is its own check, not GPTZero or similar.
Good to know

It's open source under the MIT licence, free, with no signup and no API key. The skills are plain instructions plus two small Python scripts, and neither script connects to the internet. Anthropic doesn't review outside plugins, so it's worth a quick look at the repo before you add it.
