How it works

Example · same topic, new look

Step 1 · Your content.md file

MY CONTENT: [Title of the presentation] WHAT I WANT TO SAY: 1. [The problem, in one line] 2. [Why it matters: a number, cost or example] 3. [My idea / solution] 4. [How it works: steps] 5. [Proof: result, data, customer, case study] 6. [What I want them to do next] MY NUMBERS / DATA: - [e.g. Sales Jan 4L, Feb 5L, Mar 7L] - [e.g. 3 out of 10 customers come back] MUST INCLUDE: - [logo, a specific slide, a quote, team names...] AVOID: - [words, topics or numbers not to show]

Step 2 · Free design websites

  • Slidesgo (slidesgo.com): Free templates. Download the PowerPoint version. Free templates need credit to Slidesgo, and free accounts have a monthly download limit.
  • Figma Community (figma.com/community/presentations): Modern designs, free Figma account needed. Open the file in Figma Slides, then export it as PowerPoint (.pptx). Check the export: some designs come out as flat images.
  • Microsoft PowerPoint templates (powerpoint.cloud.microsoft): Free templates from Microsoft. Open one, then download it as a .pptx.

Always get the PowerPoint (.pptx) file. If it comes as a .zip, unzip it first and upload the .pptx inside.

Step 3 · The prompt

Act as a senior presentation strategist and executive designer. I've uploaded 2 files: 1. A PowerPoint template (.pptx): use its DESIGN only (fonts, colours, backgrounds, layouts). 2. My content file: use its FACTS only. Don't invent numbers or results. If something is missing, ask me. Work in 3 stages. Stop after each stage and wait for my reply. STAGE 1: ASK ME FIRST Read both files, then ask me these questions in one numbered list. Give 2-3 example answers under each so I can reply fast: 1. Topic: what is this presentation about? 2. Audience: who will watch it? (e.g. my manager, clients, students, investors) 3. Goal: what should they do or decide after it? (e.g. approve budget, buy, learn a process) 4. Length: how many slides? 5. Tone: how should it feel? (e.g. confident, simple, data-driven, friendly) Then add up to 3 more questions about anything missing or unclear in my content file (for example key numbers, logo or brand rules, slides that must be included). Don't plan or build anything until I answer. STAGE 2: PLAN + ONE SAMPLE SLIDE After my answers, give me: 1. Core message: one sentence the audience must remember. Every slide must support this ONE point. 2. Story: Hook -> Problem -> Insight -> Solution -> Proof -> Call to action (one line each). 3. Slide-by-slide plan. For each slide: - Title written as the conclusion ("Late payments cost us 4 lakh a month", not "Payments") - Max 3 short bullet fragments - Visual: the chart, diagram, image or icon layout (e.g. "bar chart comparing X and Y", "3-step process diagram", "photo + 3 icon cards") - Template layout to use. Use different layouts from the template; never the same layout on 2 slides in a row. - Animation: what appears, in what order (e.g. "title, then bars grow one by one, then the takeaway fades in") 4. Build ONE sample slide as a .pptx (the most visual slide) in the template's theme, so I can check the look before you build the rest. STAGE 3: FULL DECK (only after I say "approved" or send changes) Build the full deck as a downloadable .pptx: - Keep the template's fonts, colours and backgrounds. Delete its sample slides and any leftover placeholder text. - Every slide has a visual: a real chart from my numbers, a diagram (process, timeline, comparison, cycle), icons or an image. No slide with only bullets. - Images: use the template's images, icons or shapes. If a photo would help and you can't add one, leave a clearly labelled image box saying exactly what photo to put there. - Animations: add entrance animations that follow the story, one point at a time, title first. If you can't add animations to the file, give me a table (slide -> object -> animation -> order) so I can add them in PowerPoint. - Text: body at least 18pt, titles at least 28pt. Nothing outside the slide, nothing overlapping. - Put the details and what I should say in the speaker notes. At the end, list anything you assumed or couldn't do.

Points to be noted

  • A paid plan gives much better results. Claude Pro (or another paid AI plan, like ChatGPT Plus) handles bigger templates, longer decks and more fixes in one chat. The free plan works, but often stops halfway on long decks because of daily limits.
  • On the free plan, keep it to 6–8 slides and a simple template. If you hit the limit, wait for it to reset and type “continue” in the same chat.
  • No file, only text? In Claude's settings, turn on file creation (code execution), then try again.
  • Animations don't always get added inside the file. Then Claude gives you an animation table. Adding them in PowerPoint takes about a minute.
  • Photos: Claude can't add photos from the internet. It uses the template's images and icons, and marks where your own photo should go.
  • Figma templates sometimes export as flat pictures that Claude can't restyle. Slidesgo and Microsoft templates are safer.
  • Use .md, not Word or PDF, for your content. Plain text uses fewer tokens, so more of your limit goes into the slides.
  • Always check numbers. The prompt tells Claude not to invent data, but read every chart once before you present.
  • Slidesgo's free templates ask you to credit them: keep their credit slide.
  • Don't upload confidential company data you aren't allowed to share.

Need help? Full steps

01

Step 1: Save your content as a .md file

Copy the content file above into any notes or text app. Replace everything in [brackets] with your own words and real numbers. Save it as content.md (a .txt file works too). A .md file is plain text, so Claude reads it with far fewer tokens than a Word file or PDF, and saves your free limit for building the slides. Real numbers become real charts.

02

Step 2: Pick and download a design

Open any of the 3 sites above, choose a template you like and get the PowerPoint (.pptx) file. Pick one with lots of different slide layouts and some charts or icons in it. More layouts means more variety in your deck.

03

Step 3: Upload both files + paste the prompt

Go to claude.ai or the Claude app, start a new chat, tap + and add both files: the template .pptx and your content.md. Then paste The prompt. Creating files works on the free plan too.

04

Step 4: Answer Claude's questions

Claude reads your files and asks you: topic, audience, goal, number of slides and tone, plus up to 3 questions about your content. It gives example answers, so just reply with the numbers and your answers in one message. Better answers give a sharper story.

05

Step 5: Check the plan and the sample slide

Claude sends the one-line core message, the story, a slide-by-slide plan with a chart or diagram for each slide, and ONE sample slide in your template's design. Open it. Don't like it? Say what to change. Like it? Reply approved.

06

Step 6: Get the full deck and check it

Claude builds the full .pptx with charts, diagrams and animations. Open it in PowerPoint, Google Slides or Keynote and read every slide once. If Claude gave you an animation table, add them in PowerPoint: Animations tab, pick the object, pick the effect. Small fixes are faster by hand. For bigger ones, send the prompt below.

Something looks off? Send this

Some slides look crowded or off-template. Recheck every slide against the template: same fonts and colours, no text overflowing, no leftover placeholder text, a visual on every slide, animations in story order. Fix them and give me the updated .pptx.
