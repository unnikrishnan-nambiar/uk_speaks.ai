See it working

Harvey finds small businesses worth selling to, learns one specific thing about each, writes a short cold email that mentions it, sends it, reads the replies and keeps the conversation going toward a meeting. It runs on your own computer, every 15 minutes, on its own.

Why the subscription thing matters

Most AI sales tools charge per token, which means for every bit of text the AI reads and writes. An agent that thinks all day burns money all day, so those tools cut corners: shorter prompts, cheaper models, less thinking.

Harvey runs on the Claude Pro or Max plan you already pay for, through Claude Code. No API key, no per-token bill. So it can afford to read each business properly and write a real email, not a template.

It also budgets itself: it checks how much of your plan's limit is used and stops early, so you still have Claude left for your own work. By default it uses at most 80%.

What it does

Five helpers, one agent

  • Scout scores the businesses it found and picks out what's specific about each.
  • Writer writes a 3-email sequence: under 75 words, no “I hope this finds you well”.
  • Sender sends at a human pace, within a daily limit, and stops the moment anyone at that company replies.
  • Handler reads replies, works out what they mean (interested, objection, not now) and drafts an answer.
  • Analyst shows what's working when things are quiet.

You choose what it looks for

Harvey can spot 29 signals about a business: no website, no online booking, running Google Ads, a stale blog, hiring for a role, which agency built their site. It collects none of them until you confirm them. Then you build a target list from them, like:

Runs Google Ads + has an agency + no online booking → 23 companies. You know exactly why each one is on the list, and so does the email.

Set it up

What you need

  • A Mac, Windows or Linux computer that can stay on while Harvey works.
  • Claude Pro or Max, with Claude Code installed and logged in.
  • Python 3.11 or newer. Claude installs it for you in step 02 if it's missing.
  • Your product's website, so Harvey can learn what you sell.
  • A separate sending domain and mailbox (Gmail/Google Workspace or Microsoft 365). Never your main domain: see “Before you send” below.
01

Step 1: Install Claude Code

Open Terminal (Mac) or PowerShell (Windows), paste the line for your computer, press Enter, then type claude and log in with your Claude account.

Mac or Linux

curl -fsSL https://claude.ai/install.sh | bash

Windows

irm https://claude.ai/install.ps1 | iex

02

Step 2: Download Harvey and open it in Claude

In the same window, paste these three lines. They download Harvey, open its folder and start Claude Code inside it.

Download and open

git clone https://github.com/ethanplusai/harvey.git cd harvey claude

No git on your computer? Start claude anywhere and say: “Download https://github.com/ethanplusai/harvey into my home folder and open it.”

03

Step 3: Say “set up Harvey for me”

Harvey comes with its own setup instructions for Claude. Claude checks what's missing, installs it, asks you questions and learns your product from your website. Give it the details up front:

Setup prompt

Set up Harvey for me. My product's website: [YOUR WEBSITE] Who I sell to: [e.g. dental clinics and salons in Kochi] Who I email there: [e.g. the owner] Mailbox to send from: [Gmail / Google Workspace / Microsoft 365] Keep approval mode on, so nothing sends without me. Leave LinkedIn off. Explain each step in simple words and ask before you install anything.

04

Step 4: Confirm the signals

Harvey shows its list of signals and what each costs. Start with the free ones (26 of 29). Ask Claude to run this, or tick them in the dashboard's Signals tab:

harvey signals --confirm free

05

Step 5: Find businesses

Run harvey discover. The default source, OpenStreetMap, is free and needs no account. It's thin for businesses without a shop front, like plumbers or roofers. A paid source shows its price and an estimate before it spends anything.

06

Step 6: Open the dashboard and start Harvey

Run harvey dashboard. It prints a private link: open it in your browser. Then run harvey run in another window. Harvey wakes every 15 minutes, decides what most needs doing, does it and goes back to sleep.

The dashboard only opens on your computer, with that private link.

07

Step 7: Approve every email in the Outbox

Every email waits in the Outbox until you approve it. Next to each one you see the company, how the address was found and the signals the email uses.

  • Keys: A approve, E edit, R reject, J/K next and previous.
  • Read each one. Don't use “approve all” on your first batches.
  • Replies show up in Conversations. When someone asks for a contract or something Harvey can't answer safely, it hands the thread to you.
  • Autopilot (sending without approval) exists. Only consider it after weeks of reading what Harvey writes.
Stop everything

Press Ctrl + C to stop Harvey, or run harvey sending pause to stop all emails right away while it keeps working. Everything is saved, so it picks up where it left off.

Before you send

Don't land in spam

  1. Buy a separate domain for sending, like getyourbrand.com, not yourbrand.com. If it gets a bad name, your main email is safe.
  2. Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC on it. Ask Claude to walk you through it, then run harvey warmup to check.
  3. Warm it up for 2–4 weeks before real volume.
  4. Start at 10–20 emails a day and add about 5 a day. Harvey can ramp up for you.
  5. Watch bounces. Above about 3%, stop and fix your list. Harvey stops itself past your limit.

Stay legal and honest

  • You are the sender, not Harvey. Check the cold email rules in the countries you email: they differ a lot.
  • Every cold email ends with an opt-out line. Anyone who says “no thanks” or “stop” is never emailed again.
  • Add your postal address in the settings before your first campaign. Harvey warns you until you do.
  • If someone asks whether they're talking to an AI, Harvey says yes. Never change that.
  • Leave LinkedIn off. Automating LinkedIn breaks its rules and can get your account restricted.
  • It runs on your own Claude plan, so check Anthropic's terms and usage limits too.

Good to know

  • Don't want to send? Use it just to find prospects, then harvey export a list.
  • Make it sound like you: its sales know-how is plain text in the skills folder. Ask Claude to change how it writes, or how it handles an objection.
  • On ChatGPT instead? It can run on a ChatGPT plan through Codex, on Mac or Linux only.
  • Your data stays on your computer. Nothing goes anywhere except the services you set up.
