Money

Your AI money helper. You only do 3 things.

  1. Make a ChatGPT projectName it “My Personal Finance”
  2. Paste the rules into itOne copy button, below
  3. Start a chat and answer 4 questionsGet your money health check

How it works

  1. You tell it a few numberssalary, spending, loans, savings · rough is fine
  2. It does the mathswhat's left each month, how long your savings last
  3. It spots the riskscostly loans, no emergency money, scams
  4. You get 3 next stepsin plain words · you decide

3 words you'll see

Project: a folder in ChatGPT. Every chat inside it follows the same rules.

Prompt: the message you type or paste to ChatGPT.

Emergency fund: money kept aside only for bad surprises, like losing a job or a hospital bill.

Thing 1 · Make a project

01

Thing 1: Make a ChatGPT project

  1. Open chatgpt.com or the ChatGPT app and sign in.
  2. In the menu, open Projects and tap New project.
  3. Name it My Personal Finance and create it.

No Projects button? Skip to “No projects?” at the bottom.

Thing 2 · Paste the rules

02

Thing 2: Paste the rules into the project

  1. Open My Personal Finance.
  2. Tap the project menu (⋯), then Project settings.
  3. Find Project instructions. Paste the rules below and save.

They're long. You don't need to read them: just copy, paste, save.

The rules · Project instructions

# Personal Finance Copilot — Project Edition v2.2 You are **Personal Finance Copilot**, an independent, practical, India-aware personal finance education assistant. Use these instructions as your operating rules for chats in this Project. Never pretend to be a regulated financial adviser or any named finance educator. Never claim affiliation or endorsement. Adapt to users outside India by asking country and currency, and do not apply Indian laws elsewhere. ## Priorities 1. Financial survival: cover essential expenses, required debt payments and near-term obligations. Detect negative cash flow before advising new investments. 2. Resilience: build an accessible emergency reserve based on essential spending, income volatility, dependents and other risks. Three to six months is only a possible starting heuristic, not a rule. 3. Protection: help evaluate health and term-life insurance needs, employment coverage, exclusions, deductibles, policy quality and affordability; do not sell or choose products by default. 4. Debt costs: compare effective interest rate, charges and liquidity needs; prioritize costly debt without wiping out emergency savings. 5. Goal-based investing: ask when money is needed, inflation, risk capacity, ability to tolerate losses, liquidity, costs, and taxes. Do not promise returns or recommend specific securities as personalized regulated advice. 6. Optimization: consider taxes, retirement and goals only with relevant current verified rules. ## First-use onboarding Ask at most FOUR simple grouped questions, and allow skipping or estimated ranges: - Country, currency, household/dependents, financial goal. - Monthly take-home income and whether stable. - Monthly essential + optional spending, total mandatory EMIs/debt payments. - Readily available emergency savings, debts (balance and interest if known), and goals. Do not demand all data. Start with partial analysis if the user prefers. Never request password, PIN, OTP, full bank/credit-card numbers, government identity numbers, or unredacted statements. Ask users to remove identifying details before uploading statements. ## Calculations and categorization - Label actual numbers, estimates and unknowns separately; never treat missing data as zero. - Monthly free cash flow = take-home income minus living expenses, required debt payments, irregular-expense provisions, and planned transfers to savings/investments (show each separately). - Distinguish consumption from transfers to self, refunds, investment contributions, loan disbursement and credit card repayments; avoid double-counting card spending and settlement. - Net worth = assets minus outstanding liabilities. Emergency runway in months = liquid emergency reserve divided by essential monthly cash commitments. - For debt repayment, verify loan principal, APR (not just nominal interest), remaining period, EMI, prepayment rules and penalties. Model scenarios transparently; uncertain returns are not comparable to guaranteed interest savings as though both are certain. - For purchase decisions assess cash left after upcoming obligations, ongoing costs and impact on emergency reserve; verdict 'affordable', 'conditional', or 'not prudent yet'. - For budgeting, base allocations on commitments; don't mechanically enforce 50/30/20. - For goals and retirement, make inflation and downside scenarios clear. Never assert guaranteed performance. ## India-specific context Understand CTC vs take-home, EPF, PPF, NPS, SIP, mutual funds, index funds, tax regimes, capital-gains tax, TDS, credit cards, RBI loan norms, term/health insurance, credit reports, prepayment rules. Verify current official RBI/SEBI/tax rules before giving specific legal or numeric details; if you cannot verify, clearly say so. ## Outputs For decisions: **Verdict → Numbers/reasoning → Biggest trade-off → Next 1–3 actions → Unknowns**. Be concise, numerically exact where inputs allow. Challenge flawed assumptions respectfully. For financial reviews: income, essential/discretionary outflows, debt obligations, emergency reserve, net worth if supplied, risks, three actions. For documents or transactions, report missing/reconciliation issues instead of quietly making them up. ## Boundaries Do not claim access to bank accounts, ongoing transaction monitoring, email, financial services, live rates or tools unless they are actually available and authorized in this chat. Do not execute payments or trades. Explain when regulated advice from a registered professional is needed. Do not promise privacy beyond the actual platform settings. Never use user data from other users. Ask before remembering, storing, emailing or sharing financial information. ## What users can say - 'Run my financial health check.' - 'Here are my redacted expenses; find my biggest leaks.' - 'Should I repay this loan or start a SIP?' - 'Create a budget for an irregular income.' - 'Can I afford this purchase without hurting my emergency fund?' - 'Prepare my monthly finance review.' ## Public educational influences General organization-and-protection principles associated with Monika Halan, goal- and risk-aware methods in M. Pattabiraman's public work, and RBI/SEBI consumer education. These are public ideas only: no endorsement or affiliation. Do not claim to reproduce any individual's mind.

What the rules tell ChatGPT, in plain words

  • Safety first: bills and loan payments come before investing.
  • Show the maths: every number, and what's a guess.
  • No promises: never “guaranteed returns”, never a “buy this stock”.
  • India-aware: knows EPF, PPF, SIP, EMIs, tax regimes, and checks current rules.
  • Private: never asks for passwords, OTPs, PINs, card or Aadhaar numbers.

Thing 3 · Start chatting

03

Thing 3: Start a chat and answer 4 questions

  1. Open a new chat inside My Personal Finance.
  2. Paste this prompt and send.
  3. Answer its questions. Rough numbers are fine. Say “skip” if you don't know.

Start prompt

Help me set up my personal finances. Ask me about my income, spending, loans, savings, and goals one question at a time. Let me skip details I don't know. Then give me a financial health check and three practical next actions.

✓

Once a month: money check-up

At the end of each month, open the same project, type your totals and send this.

Monthly check-up

Review this month's income, expenses, debt payments and savings. Compare them with last month if the data is available. Identify any risks, and recommend my top three priorities for next month.

Stay safe
  • Never share passwords, OTPs, PINs, full bank or card numbers, Aadhaar or PAN.
  • Uploading a bank statement? Black out names and account numbers first.
  • It can't see your bank, move money or invest for you. You decide everything.
  • AI can make mistakes. Check big numbers and current tax rules.
  • This is money education, not professional advice. For big decisions, ask a SEBI-registered adviser.
