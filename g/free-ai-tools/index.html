01 PPT

Gamma

Type a topic and Gamma makes a full, designed presentation in about a minute: slides, layout, images. Edit it like a document, then download it as PowerPoint or PDF.

  1. Sign up with Google at gamma.app.
  2. Tap Create new → Generate and choose Presentation.
  3. Paste the prompt, pick the number of slides, review the outline, then generate.
  4. Change anything by typing (“make slide 3 simpler”), then Export to PowerPoint or PDF.
  • gamma.appOpen Gamma

Free plan gives a set of credits to try it; downloads may carry Gamma branding.

PPT prompt

Make a [10]-slide presentation on: [TOPIC] Audience: [e.g. my college class / my team / a client] Goal: by the end, they should [UNDERSTAND / DECIDE / BUY] [WHAT] One idea per slide, short bullet points, a clear title on every slide. Slide 1: the title and a one-line hook. Last slide: a summary and one next step. Add simple visuals, no clip art.

02 Posters

Gemini / ChatGPT

Both can now make posters with readable text: event posters, offers for your shop, birthday invites, YouTube thumbnails. Describe it, and fix it by chatting.

  1. Open Gemini or ChatGPT (both work on the free plan).
  2. Paste the prompt with your details filled in.
  3. Fix it by chatting: “bigger date”, “change the colour to green”, “use this logo” (attach it).
  4. Download, then check every word and number before you print or post.
  • gemini.google.comOpen Gemini
  • chatgpt.comOpen ChatGPT

Free plans have a daily limit on images. AI still misspells sometimes: always check the text.

Posters prompt

Create a poster, [PORTRAIT 4:5 for Instagram / A4 for print]. What: [EVENT / OFFER / ANNOUNCEMENT] Headline: "[MAIN TEXT]" Details: [DATE, TIME, PLACE, PRICE, PHONE] Style: [e.g. clean and modern / festive Onam colours / bold black and white] Make the headline big and easy to read from far away. Use exactly the text I gave, spelled correctly. No extra text.

03 Video

Google Flow

Google's AI video studio, powered by its Veo model. Describe a scene and it makes a short video clip with sound. Join clips into a longer scene.

  1. Open Flow and sign in with your Google account.
  2. Start a new project and choose Text to video.
  3. Paste the prompt. Describe one shot at a time: who, where, what happens, the camera.
  4. Download the clips you like and join them in CapCut, or extend them in Flow.
  • labs.google/fx/tools/flowOpen Google Flow

Free access gives a small number of credits each month; Google AI plans give more. Availability depends on your country.

Video prompt

An 8-second video, [VERTICAL 9:16]. Scene: [WHERE, e.g. a small tea shop in Kochi, early morning] Subject: [WHO OR WHAT, e.g. an old man pouring tea from a height] Action: [WHAT HAPPENS] Camera: [e.g. slow push-in, close-up, eye level] Light and mood: [e.g. warm sunrise light, calm] Sound: [e.g. rain outside, cups clinking, no music] Realistic, natural movement. No text on screen.

04 Interview

ChatGPT Voice

Talk to ChatGPT out loud and it becomes your interviewer: it asks questions from the real job description, follows up, and gives you a feedback report at the end.

  1. Open the ChatGPT app and make a project with your CV and the job description.
  2. Paste the prompt below as the project's instructions.
  3. Start a chat in the project, tap the voice icon and say “Hello”.
  4. When it ends, type REPORT for your feedback. Fix the top 3 things and go again.
  • chatgpt.comOpen ChatGPT Voice

Voice mode and projects work on the free plan, with daily limits.

Full step-by-step interview guide →

Interview prompt

You are my interviewer for a mock interview. My CV and the job description are in this project's files. When I say "Hello", greet me and start. Ask [8] questions, one at a time: a warm-up, 3 about my CV, 3 about the job, and 1 tough one. Ask one follow-up if an answer is vague. No feedback until the end. At the end say: "End voice mode and type REPORT." When I type REPORT, give me a score out of 10, what worked, what was missing in each answer, a stronger answer in STAR format using only my real experience, and the top 3 things to fix.

05 Big PDF

NotebookLM

Upload big PDFs, notes, slides or YouTube links, and ask questions about them. It answers only from your files, with the page it came from, so it doesn't make things up. It can also turn them into a podcast-style audio summary.

  1. Open NotebookLM and sign in with Google. Tap New notebook.
  2. Add your sources: PDFs, Google Docs, website or YouTube links.
  3. Ask questions in the chat. Tap the numbers in the answer to see the exact source.
  4. Try Audio overview to listen to a summary on the go, or ask for a study guide.
  • notebooklm.google.comOpen NotebookLM

Free with a Google account, with limits on notebooks and sources.

Big PDF prompt

Using only these sources: 1. Summarise the whole thing in 10 bullet points, in simple English. 2. List the 10 most important facts, numbers or definitions, with the page for each. 3. Make 10 practice questions with answers, from easy to hard. 4. What do the sources NOT cover that I might expect them to?

06 Website

Lovable

Describe the website you want in plain English and Lovable builds it, live, in minutes. Change it by chatting, then publish it with one click. No coding.

  1. Sign up at lovable.dev.
  2. Paste the prompt with your business details into the box on the home page.
  3. Fix it by chatting: “make the phone number a WhatsApp button”, “add our menu”.
  4. Tap Publish to get a live link. Check it on your phone before you share it.
  • lovable.devOpen Lovable

Free plan gives a few credits a day, enough to build and publish a simple site.

Website prompt

Build a one-page website for my business. Business: [NAME], a [WHAT YOU DO] in [PLACE] For: [WHO YOUR CUSTOMERS ARE] Sections: a hero with a one-line promise, services with prices, why choose us, 3 reviews, location with a map, and contact. Contact: a big WhatsApp button to [NUMBER] and a call button. Style: [e.g. clean, white and green, friendly] Mobile first: most visitors are on phones. Simple words, fast to load. Use placeholder images I can replace.

07 CV & Email

Claude

The best of the three for writing that sounds like a real person: CVs that get past ATS software, cover letters, and the emails you've been putting off.

  1. Open Claude (app or claude.ai) and start a new chat.
  2. For a CV: attach your CV and the job description, then use the CV guide's prompt.
  3. For an email: paste the prompt below with the situation filled in.
  4. Read it once and change anything that doesn't sound like you before you send.
  • claude.aiOpen Claude

Free plan works, with a daily message limit.

Full CV guide: get past the ATS →

CV & Email prompt

Write an email for me. To: [WHO, e.g. my manager / a client / HR] Situation: [WHAT HAPPENED] What I want: [THE OUTCOME, e.g. a refund / a meeting / more time] Tone: [polite but firm / warm / formal] Keep it under 120 words. Clear subject line. Say what I want in the first two lines. No "I hope this email finds you well". Give me 2 versions: one shorter, one more formal.

About “free”

All 7 have a free plan, with limits: a few credits, a daily cap, or a watermark. That's plenty to learn and to do real work now and then. Limits change often, so check inside each app. Never pay for a tool until the free plan has saved you time.

Use them safely

  • Don't upload ID cards, bank documents or your company's confidential files.
  • Check every fact, number and spelling before you share or print.
  • Sign in with Google where you can, and turn on 2-step verification.
